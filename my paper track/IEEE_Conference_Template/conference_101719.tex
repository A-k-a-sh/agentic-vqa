\begin{abstract}
Knowledge-Based Visual Question Answering (KB-VQA) requires external world knowledge to answer open-ended questions grounded in visual entities. While state-of-the-art architectures design multi-stage retrieval pipelines to search massive encyclopedic corpora, they suffer from a fundamental architectural flaw: rigidity. Queries traverse fixed, sequential stages---coarse search, multimodal reranking, and generation---without uncertainty detection or adaptive branching. Through an extensive empirical audit of 1,000 queries on the InfoSeek benchmark against the full 100,000-article Wikipedia knowledge base, we uncover that 40.8\% of all pipeline errors stem not from retrieval absence, but from premature filtering of valid candidates already present in the candidate pool. Furthermore, we discover that ambiguous routing instances exhibit a sharp 3.3$\times$ collapse in the multimodal reranking score margin ($\Delta = 0.0512 \to 0.0155$). Leveraging this signal, we propose Adaptive Agentic Retrieval, a framework that isolates high-risk queries using a zero-cost threshold ($\tau = 0.025$) to trigger dynamic verification and context expansion. We demonstrate that off-the-shelf zero-shot vision-language models fail as adjudicators due to severe positional primacy bias (17.5\%), reducing recovery to a sub-random coin-flip. By introducing symmetric parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT), we eliminate positional bias to 1.33\% and unlock genuine error recovery. Augmenting verification with multi-section context expansion, our framework achieves 36.30\% VQA accuracy (+1.40\% over the OMGM baseline, rescuing 44 previously failed queries) under strict zero ground-truth leakage, establishing a new state of the art.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Visual Question Answering (VQA) has progressively advanced from closed-world perceptual reasoning over static images to open-world Knowledge-Based Visual Question Answering (KB-VQA)~\cite{okvqa, infoseek, oven}. In KB-VQA, answering questions about real-world entities (e.g., identifying obscure cultural landmarks, rare biological taxa, or historical monuments) requires systems to retrieve factual knowledge from external web-scale corpora, such as Wikipedia. Unlike conventional text-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), multimodal knowledge retrieval requires cross-modal alignment between visual entity appearance and dense textual descriptions across millions of candidate articles.

To address this challenge, state-of-the-art architectures, epitomized by the OMGM framework~\cite{omgm2025}, decompose the retrieval process into a multi-stage sequential cascade: (1) coarse multimodal retrieval to fetch candidate entities from an encyclopedic index (e.g., using CLIP visual embeddings against Wikipedia summaries), (2) fine-grained multimodal reranking using cross-attention models (e.g., Q-Former image-text matching~\cite{blip2}) to score entity relevance, and (3) passage-level section reranking followed by answer generation using large Vision-Language Models (VLMs)~\cite{llava15}.

Despite the sophistication of individual components, existing KB-VQA pipelines suffer from a fundamental architectural flaw: \textbf{rigid, irreversible execution}. In conventional architectures, test queries traverse a fixed single-pass pipeline where only the top-1 ranked candidate at each stage is forwarded to the next. If the multimodal reranker assigns the ground-truth entity to rank~2 instead of rank~1 by an infinitesimal margin, that entity is permanently discarded. The downstream generation model is subsequently forced to answer using an incorrect entity's text, triggering catastrophic error propagation. Existing pipelines operate without mechanisms for uncertainty detection, confidence calibration, verification, or adaptive branching.

To determine where multi-stage retrieval pipelines fail in practice, we conducted an exhaustive evidence-first failure audit across 1,000 queries on the InfoSeek benchmark evaluated against the full, unpruned 100,000-article Wikipedia knowledge base. Our empirical audit reveals that \textbf{40.8\% of all pipeline failures (408 queries) stem directly from this rigid ranking bottleneck} (Category~B: 20.5\% entity reranking errors and Category~C: 20.3\% section selection errors). That is, in over 40\% of the benchmark, the required factual knowledge was already successfully retrieved in the candidate pool, but prematurely filtered out by the static pipeline. Furthermore, generator failure alone (Category~D) accounts for only 13.7\% of errors, and LLaVA-1.5-7B generates the correct answer 64.7\% of the time when provided the true entity and section. These findings provide definitive empirical proof that retrieval performance is not constrained by knowledge absence or generator capacity, but by rigid, single-pass routing.

Can we dynamically detect when a pipeline is susceptible to a routing failure? By analyzing multimodal reranking scores, we discover a striking mathematical signature: when the top-1 candidate prediction is correct, the score gap between the first and second candidates ($\Delta = S_1 - S_2$) is substantial ($\Delta_{avg} = 0.0512$). Conversely, when an entity reranking failure occurs, this margin collapses by $3.3\times$ to $\Delta_{avg} = 0.0155$. This pronounced signal enables a zero-cost mathematical threshold ($\tau = 0.025$) that isolates ambiguous, high-risk queries without imposing computational overhead on confident queries.

However, resolving ambiguous queries requires a reliable visual verification tool. Crucially, our investigations uncover a severe \textbf{verification dilemma}: off-the-shelf zero-shot VLMs are incapable of serving as visual adjudicators. In zero-shot pairwise disambiguation, models exhibit catastrophic formatting collapse (47.5\% unparseable responses) and severe positional primacy bias (17.5\%), yielding a sub-random accuracy of 32.5\% and an effective decision win-rate of only 45.6\%--47.2\%---virtually identical to a random coin-flip. Heuristic routing policies alone cannot overcome an uncalibrated zero-shot foundation model.

To resolve this limitation, we present \textbf{Adaptive Agentic Retrieval}, a framework that pairs deterministic, zero-cost score-margin gating with calibrated verification tools. Rather than deploying opaque reinforcement learning controllers, our control flow remains fully transparent: confident queries ($\Delta \ge 0.025$) bypass routing with zero overhead, while ambiguous queries ($\Delta < 0.025$) trigger dynamic verification. To overcome the zero-shot verification trap, we apply symmetric Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT, LoRA $r=16$) to the verifier, virtually eliminating positional bias (reduced to 1.33\%) and raising disambiguation accuracy to 90.33\%. Furthermore, we tackle section-level evidence omissions (Category~C) through multi-section context expansion, uncovering a fundamental double dissociation: lightweight 7B models establish the Pareto frontier for pairwise verification, whereas larger 13B models are structurally necessary to withstand distractor dilution during multi-section evidence extraction.

The primary contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Empirical Failure Audit \& Score Margin Discovery:} We provide the first comprehensive failure taxonomy on the InfoSeek benchmark against the full 100k Wikipedia corpus, demonstrating that 40.8\% of errors represent candidate-pool retention bottlenecks. We uncover a $3.3\times$ score margin collapse ($\Delta = 0.0512 \to 0.0155$) that enables zero-cost ambiguity isolation.
    \item \textbf{Symmetric PEFT Tool Calibration:} We expose the failure of off-the-shelf zero-shot VLMs in agentic verification ($45.6\%$--$47.2\%$ precision) and demonstrate that minimal symmetric LoRA adaptation on balanced entity pairs eliminates positional primacy bias ($17.5\% \to 1.33\%$), unlocking net-positive error recovery.
    \item \textbf{The $2 \times 2$ Capacity \& Verification Double Dissociation:} We uncover a fundamental trade-off between model scale and task formulation: lightweight 7B models are Pareto-optimal for pairwise entity verification (+1.10\% net accuracy, 62.8\% win-rate at 44\% lower latency), whereas larger 13B models are required (+1.40\% net accuracy, rescuing 44 queries) to resist distractor dilution during multi-section context expansion.
    \item \textbf{Zero-Leakage Benchmark Superiority:} Evaluated on the 1,000 InfoSeek test queries with strict zero ground-truth leakage and official metrics, our champion model achieves 36.30\% VQA accuracy (+1.40\% over the OMGM baseline, rescuing 44 failed queries), establishing a new state of the art in adaptive multimodal retrieval.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Knowledge-Based Visual Question Answering}
Visual Question Answering (VQA) has matured from perceptual scene interpretation~\cite{vqa_v2} to Knowledge-Based VQA (KB-VQA)~\cite{okvqa, aokvqa}, where models must access open-world external knowledge bases to answer entity-centric questions. Benchmark datasets such as OVEN~\cite{oven} and InfoSeek~\cite{infoseek} expanded this paradigm to entity linking across millions of Wikipedia articles. To bridge visual queries with vast unstructured corpora, recent state-of-the-art frameworks employ multi-stage retrieval pipelines. Most notably, the OMGM framework~\cite{omgm2025} orchestrates multiple granularities and modalities: first executing coarse candidate search via CLIP visual embeddings~\cite{clip} against an encyclopedic FAISS index, followed by fine-grained multimodal reranking using BLIP-2 Q-Former cross-attention~\cite{blip2}, and finally section reranking via BGE-reranker-v2-m3~\cite{bge_reranker} combined with LLaVA answer generation~\cite{llava15}. However, all such multi-stage pipelines enforce rigid, unidirectional execution: top candidates are passed downstream irrevocably, causing early ranking misclassifications to cascade into permanent answer failures without mechanisms for uncertainty detection or verification.

\subsection{Agentic, Adaptive, and Self-Reflective Retrieval}
In natural language processing, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has increasingly embraced adaptive and agentic paradigms to overcome the limitations of static single-pass retrieval. Architectures such as Self-RAG~\cite{selfrag}, Corrective RAG (CRAG)~\cite{crag}, and Adaptive-RAG~\cite{adaptiverag} dynamically gauge retrieval necessity, verify retrieved documents, and formulate iterative search queries. Similarly, generalist agent frameworks like ReAct~\cite{react} and Toolformer~\cite{toolformer} empower language models to interleave reasoning with tool execution. Extending agentic retrieval to multimodal settings has primarily relied on black-box LLM planners or open-ended multi-turn dialog agents. While expressive, relying on general-purpose LLM routers introduces severe latency overhead, high operational costs, and unpredictable routing behaviors. In contrast, our approach preserves a deterministic, zero-cost mathematical threshold ($\tau = 0.025$) on intrinsic multimodal reranking margins, activating targeted visual verification tools only when uncertainty is mathematically detected.

\subsection{VLM Calibration, Positional Primacy Bias, and PEFT}
Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated impressive multimodal comprehension across diverse zero-shot tasks~\cite{llava15, blip2}. Nonetheless, recent studies have demonstrated that foundation VLMs suffer from acute calibration flaws, hallucination, and severe order-sensitivity biases~\cite{primacy_bias, peiss_order_bias}. In comparative pairwise evaluation (e.g., adjudicating between Candidate A and Candidate B), zero-shot VLMs exhibit pronounced \textit{positional primacy bias}, disproportionately favoring the first-presented option regardless of visual alignment. When deployed as zero-shot verification agents, this bias leads to catastrophic decision failure. To address these vulnerabilities without costly full-parameter retraining, Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT), particularly Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)~\cite{lora} and QLoRA~\cite{qlora}, offers a mechanism to calibrate specific model behaviors. We demonstrate that task-specific symmetric PEFT adaptation on balanced pairwise permutations is structurally essential to neutralize positional primacy bias and convert off-the-shelf VLMs into reliable visual adjudicators.

\section{Empirical Motivation \& Failure Taxonomy}

\subsection{Reproduction of Multi-Stage KB-VQA Baselines}
To identify where state-of-the-art KB-VQA architectures fail, we reproduced the complete OMGM pipeline~\cite{omgm2025} under standardized conditions. Our evaluation was conducted on a representative test split of 1,000 queries from the InfoSeek benchmark~\cite{infoseek}, keeping the full 100,000-article Wikipedia knowledge base completely unpruned to preserve realistic retrieval complexity. The pipeline comprises:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Step 1 (Coarse Search):} Query image embeddings generated by CLIP (ViT-B/32) query a FAISS index of 100,000 Wikipedia entity summaries, retrieving the top-20 candidate entities.
    \item \textbf{Step 2 (Fine-Grained Multimodal Reranking):} Top-20 candidates are reranked via a pretrained Q-Former IT2IT model~\cite{blip2, omgm2025} that fuses cross-attention over query images, candidate Wikipedia entity images, and section texts, producing blended multimodal scores with $\alpha = 0.9$.
    \item \textbf{Step 3 (Section Reranking and VQA Generation):} Candidate sections from the top-1 entity are scored using BAAI/BGE-reranker-v2-m3~\cite{bge_reranker} fused with Step 2 section similarities ($\beta = 0.2$). The top-ranked section is provided as context to LLaVA-1.5-7B~\cite{llava15} for greedy zero-shot answer generation.
\end{enumerate}
Using the official OMGM evaluation protocol (multi-alias string matching with $\pm 10\%$ numerical tolerance), the baseline achieves an end-to-end VQA accuracy of \textbf{34.90\%} (349 / 1,000 queries), faithfully matching the published OMGM performance characteristics.

\subsection{Phase 0 Evidence-First Failure Taxonomy}
We executed an exhaustive failure audit across all 1,000 test queries, tracing the lineage of ground-truth entities and factual evidence across every stage of the pipeline. The resulting taxonomy classifies every query into one of four mutually exclusive categories:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Baseline Success (34.90\%, 349 queries):} The correct entity and relevant evidence section were selected, and the generator produced the correct factual answer.
    \item \textbf{Category A: Retrieval Ceiling Error (15.30\%, 153 queries):} The ground-truth Wikipedia entity was not present within the top-20 candidates retrieved by Step~1 coarse search. In these instances, external knowledge is completely absent from the candidate pool.
    \item \textbf{Category B: Entity Reranking Error (20.50\%, 205 queries):} The correct Wikipedia entity was successfully retrieved in the top-20 candidate pool by Step~1, but Step~2 multimodal reranking ranked it at position $> 1$, forwarding an incorrect entity to the generator.
    \item \textbf{Category C: Section Selection Error (20.30\%, 203 queries):} The correct entity was successfully ranked at top-1 by Step~2, but the section selection mechanism in Step~3 chose a section that omitted the requisite factual evidence (e.g., selecting the general lead section rather than specific dimension or biographical tables).
    \item \textbf{Category D: Generator Reasoning Error (13.70\%, 137 queries):} Both the correct entity and the correct evidence-bearing section were provided to the generator, but the model hallucinated or failed to parse the text correctly.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Key Scientific Insights and Observations}
The empirical taxonomy displayed in Fig.~\ref{fig:failure_taxonomy} reveals critical findings that challenge traditional assumptions in multimodal retrieval:

\textbf{1. The Rigid Ranking Bottleneck (Category B + C = 40.8\%):} In 408 out of 1,000 queries, the pipeline already had the required Wikipedia entity or knowledge within its grasp. However, the rigid feed-forward architecture irreversibly pruned valid candidates at either the entity level (205 queries) or section level (203 queries). This proves that premature filtering, rather than retrieval absence, is the primary performance bottleneck in state-of-the-art KB-VQA.

\textbf{2. Generator Reasoning is Not the Primary Bottleneck:} Category~D errors account for only 13.70\% of queries. When provided with the correct entity and evidence-bearing section, LLaVA-1.5-7B succeeds \textbf{64.7\%} of the time (251 correct out of 388 instances with perfect context). This indicates that scaling generation models to massive 70B parameter models is largely unnecessary if retrieval routing can be dynamically corrected.

\textbf{3. The $3.3\times$ Score Margin Signal ($\Delta = S_1 - S_2$):} When comparing the top-two multimodal reranking scores ($S_1$ and $S_2$), we observe a profound divergence in confidence margins, plotted in Fig.~\ref{fig:failure_taxonomy}(b). When the top-1 entity is correct, the score margin is wide: $\Delta_{avg} = 0.0512$. When a Category~B reranking failure occurs, the margin collapses to $\Delta_{avg} = 0.0155$---a \textbf{$3.3\times$ drop}. This provides an intrinsic, zero-cost mathematical indicator: queries with $\Delta < \tau = 0.025$ are disproportionately error-prone (exhibiting a baseline accuracy of only 25.39\%, compared to 44.72\% for queries with $\Delta \ge 0.025$). Consequently, $\tau = 0.025$ serves as an ideal gating threshold to trigger adaptive verification.
